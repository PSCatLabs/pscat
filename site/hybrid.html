pscat with an image model

An image model paints the atmosphere; pscat sets everything exact on top: type, numbers, a chart, a QR code that scans, a print-ready PDF. The image is generated once and frozen, and all the iterating happens in the pscat layer. This page is the summary; the full workflow, the prompts and the checks are in the guide. It complements When to use pscat.

Two worked examples

The comparison

The image model was also asked for each whole poster, with the same text, numbers and "a scannable QR code" for the same URL: one attempt each, no picking. Each sheet shows (a) that image, (b) pscat alone and (c) the hybrid, with checked facts under each panel.

  • Text and numbers: the image model got every word and number right in both images (read at full size against the brief), and its chart bars were within 2% of the data. pscat's text is checked string by string in its tests, and its chart is computed.
  • QR codes: the image model's did not decode, in both attempts. Two independent decoders find each symbol and fail its error correction. pscat's decode to the URL in both decoders, from the PNG and from the PDF.
  • Output: the image model gives one raster, about 127 pixels per inch at A4. The hybrid's type, chart and QR code are vectors in the PDF.
  • Looks: the layout is pscat's in (b) and (c): a grid, a display title face, a typeset formula, a chart at full size, a price column with leaders. The hybrid adds the image model's painting, which pscat's own vector art does not match for painted light.

Is it right every time?

An image model is sampled, so one good image is one sample. On 2026-11-05 we ran the whole-poster and whole-menu briefs six more times each, and a denser brief, a ferry timetable with 40 times and 8 fares, five times. Every string and number was read on 2x crops, and every QR code was run through two decoders.

  • Text and numbers: right in all 17 images: every word, every price and all 200 of the timetable runs' times. The bar charts were within 5% in five poster runs out of six, and up to 15% off in the sixth.
  • QR codes: 0 of 17 decoded.
  • A one-line edit ("change only the date") came back right, but the model redrew the whole image: 42% of the poster's other pixels changed, so it needs proofreading again.

When it is worth it

When a page needs both a painted look and something exact: a QR code, numbers from data, a page that will be edited or made in versions, or print at size. A price change is one edit and a re-render; asking the image model again repaints the whole page, which then needs proofreading again. For a mood image with nothing exact in it, use the image model alone; for a page with nothing painted, pscat alone.
